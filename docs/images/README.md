# Living Water System — Diagram Index

This directory contains conceptual engineering sketches documenting the development of the Living Water System and its component systems.

The drawings are intended to communicate architecture, operating hypotheses, component relationships, and the evolution of the design. They are not construction documents, certified engineering specifications, or validated hydraulic models.

The numbered filenames provide a readable navigation order. The numbering identifies the documentation sequence and should not, by itself, be interpreted as a final construction or operating sequence.

## 01 — Original Water Elevator Concept

**File:** `01_stage1_original_water_elevator.jpeg`

Early Stage 1 concept showing the use of ocean-wave pressure oscillation to incrementally elevate seawater into elevated storage.

This concept preceded and informed the later helical water-elevator configuration.

## 02 — Trompe Protocol

**File:** `02_stage2_trompe_protocol.jpeg`

Concept for using elevated water from the water elevator to entrain and compress air through a trompe-type hydraulic air-compression process.

The compressed air is intended as a reusable energy medium for downstream pumping and other pneumatic applications.

## 03 — Desert Whale Pump Station

**File:** `03_stage3_desert_whale_pump_station.jpeg`

Concept for an air-displacement pumping chamber powered by compressed air produced upstream.

The drawing illustrates the pump chamber, pressure cycle, float-operated control, relief pathway, and pressurized-water discharge.

## 04 — Gastropod Composite Pipe

**File:** `04_stage4_gastropod_composite_pipe.jpeg`

Biomimetic composite-pipe concept inspired by the layered protective structure of the scaly-foot gastropod.

The proposed configuration combines a vitrified clay or ceramic-lined core with a ductile-iron outer shell.

The manufacturing process remains an engineering-development question, including thermal compatibility, bonding, differential expansion, thermal shock, fabrication sequence, and production tolerances.

## 05 — Hibah Rooftop Desalination Unit

**File:** `05_hibah_rooftop_desal_unit.jpeg`

Concept for a modular rooftop desalination unit using solar energy and gravity.

The drawing emphasizes the brine-distribution and shallow sheet-flow portion of the concept, including a hydraulic level spreader, modular coconut-coir media, gravity drainage, and residual-brine flow.

The broader Hibah concept also includes evaporation, freshwater recovery through condensation, and management or potential reuse of the remaining concentrated material.

## 06 — Helical Water Elevator: Initial Side View

**File:** `06_helical_water_elevator_initial_side_view.jpeg`

Early side-view concept for the helical water elevator.

The drawing separates the wave-driven pressure plume from the seawater intake and shows the helical lift path leading toward elevated storage.

The pressure plume is intended to convert wave motion into alternating pressure conditions. A separate intake supplies the water being incrementally elevated.

The concept draws inspiration from observable natural hydraulic behavior while remaining an engineered hypothesis requiring analysis and testing.

## 07 — Helical Water Elevator: Nested Plan View

**File:** `07_helical_water_elevator_nested_plan_view.jpeg`

Plan-view sketch showing the nested system architecture.

The drawing illustrates the spatial relationship among the storage tank, tower, helical elevator, central open shaft, air-pressure connection, and water-intake connection.

## 08 — Helical Water Elevator: Unrolled Daisy Chain

**File:** `08_helical_water_elevator_unrolled_daisy_chain.jpeg`

The helical elevator concept spatially “unwrapped” into a linear sequence.

This view is intended to make the repeated cup-and-pipe ratchet architecture easier to understand before the same sequence is wrapped around the tower as a helix.

The helix therefore represents the physical arrangement of a repeated hydraulic sequence rather than a different operating principle.

## 09 — Single Elevator Hydrostatic Hold Geometry

**File:** `09_single_elevator_hydrostatic_hold_geometry.jpeg`

Detail concept for the geometry of an individual water-elevator cup.

The intended resting condition is a stable hydrostatic water level between pressure pulses so that transferred water is retained primarily by system geometry rather than requiring one-way valves to continuously support the full static water column.

The valves are intended primarily to control transient flow direction during the ratcheting pressure cycle.

This operating hypothesis requires hydraulic analysis and physical testing.

## 10 — Helical Water Elevator: Opened Component View

**File:** `10_helical_water_elevator_opened_component_view.jpeg`

Opened component view showing repeated elevator cups, one-way valves, central pressure connections, and the directional water-transfer path before the assembly is wrapped into its helical configuration.

This view emphasizes the repeated modular architecture and intended one-direction ratcheting behavior.

## Development Status

The Living Water System is an evolving open-source engineering research concept.

Known uncertainties and unresolved engineering questions are intentionally not hidden. They are part of the development record and should be investigated through analysis, modeling, experimentation, field experience, and independent criticism.

Examples include:

- hydraulic calibration of the wave-driven water elevator;
- valve behavior and hydrostatic resting conditions;
- intake filtration and exclusion of particulate matter and living organisms;
- biofouling and cleaning of wetted components;
- flushing, inspection, isolation, and maintainability;
- manufacturing of the gastropod composite pipe;
- compressed-air moisture management;
- compressed-air transmission losses and leakage;
- materials durability and corrosion;
- Hibah evaporation, condensation, brine handling, and material recovery;
- system efficiency, scalability, and lifecycle performance.

These and future questions may be tracked separately through repository documentation and GitHub Issues.

The objective is not to present a finished system.

The objective is to make the concepts sufficiently clear, auditable, and accessible that they can be examined, challenged, tested, improved, adapted, or rejected on engineering grounds.
